## Scenario

You're a backend engineer at a rapidly growing SaaS company. Your primary product manages customer orders and their associated line items. The `Order` table, which holds millions of records, has a `customer_id` and a `created_at` timestamp. Each order can have many `LineItem` records, stored in a separate table, linked by `order_id`.

A new feature requires displaying, for any given customer, their *N* most recent orders, along with the total quantity of a specific product (`product_sku: "PROD-XYZ"`) across all line items within those *N* orders. This display needs to be fast—ideally under 100ms for customers with hundreds of thousands of orders. Currently, retrieving all orders for a customer and then filtering/aggregating line items is unacceptably slow for larger customers, often taking several seconds. The `LineItem` table is also growing quickly, now exceeding 100 million rows.

## Requirements

The existing database schema cannot be changed (i.e., no adding new columns to existing tables). You can, however, introduce new tables, materialized views, or explore alternative data storage and indexing strategies. The solution must be robust to concurrent writes of new orders and line items. The system processes thousands of new orders and tens of thousands of new line items per minute. The primary constraint is latency for the read operation, especially as the number of orders per customer grows. Multi-tenancy means each `customer_id` is unique and represents a distinct customer.

## Deliverables

1.  A proposed database query (or sequence of queries) to efficiently retrieve the *N* most recent orders for a given `customer_id`, along with the aggregated quantity of `PROD-XYZ` across those *N* orders. Assume *N* can range from 1 to 50.
2.  A description of any new database objects (tables, indexes, materialized views, etc.) you would create to support this query and why they are necessary.
3.  An explanation of how you would ensure data consistency for the aggregated quantity, considering the high write volume to `Order` and `LineItem` tables.
4.  An analysis of the time complexity of your proposed solution for a single customer query as the number of orders for that customer increases.

## Going further
