## Scenario

Your company operates a popular social media platform where users can post short text updates ("posts") and follow other users. You're preparing for a major marketing push that is expected to triple user engagement. One of the core features is the "feed" — when a user logs in, they see a chronologically ordered list of posts from the users they follow. This feed is currently generated by fetching all posts from followed users and sorting them on the fly.

The current `posts` table has grown to 500 million rows, with an average of 100 posts per user. The `followers` table is even larger, with an average user following 500 other users. Generating a user's feed now frequently takes 2-3 seconds, especially for users who follow many popular accounts. During peak times, this can spike to 5-10 seconds, leading to a poor user experience and increased database load. Your current infrastructure is PostgreSQL for primary data storage, Redis for caching, and Python/Django for the backend API.

## Requirements

Design a system to reduce the average feed generation time for a user to under 200 milliseconds, even during peak load, while ensuring posts appear in chronological order. Consider the implications for data consistency, system complexity, and operational overhead. The solution should be scalable to handle a 3x increase in read traffic and a 2x increase in write traffic (new posts). The existing `posts` and `followers` tables cannot be fundamentally re-architected in a way that requires a full data migration in the scope of this exercise.

## Deliverables

1.  A high-level architectural diagram (text description or ASCII art is fine) outlining the components involved and their interactions.
2.  Describe the data flow for both a user publishing a new post and a user fetching their feed.
3.  Explain how data consistency (e.g., a post appearing in a follower's feed shortly after it's published) is maintained within acceptable limits.
4.  Identify potential bottlenecks in your proposed system and how they would be mitigated.

## Going further

How would your design change if the requirement was not just chronological order, but also a ranking algorithm that prioritizes posts based on engagement (likes, comments) from the last hour, in addition to recency?
